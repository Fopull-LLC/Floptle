# Floptle v0.19.0 — "Cut It Out"

The ⬢ Map tool grew a knife, learned to box-select from anywhere, put its
sub-object modes on screen — and blockout geometry now takes the paint brush.
Plus the bug that made per-face materials impossible to actually assign.

## The texture dropdown that shut the instant you opened it

Assigning a texture to a face material's slot didn't work. The dropdown opened
and closed on the same frame, every time, so the one control the whole per-face
material feature exists for could not be used.

Nothing was wrong with the dropdown. Every picker inside the material editor was
identified by an **absolute** id — `"mat_tex"` — and the material editor is drawn
in more than one place at once: the Inspector's Material section and the Map
tab's per-slot block live in different dock panels, so both are on screen
together whenever you're texturing a face. Two widgets, one popup id: opening
either one drew two popups under that id, and each one counted the click that
opened it as a click *outside* the other. They shut each other.

Pickers are now identified relative to the Ui they're drawn in, so each call site
gets its own. This also fixes the same collision between two slots of the same
mesh.

## ✂ Knife

`/` arms it. Click one **edge or corner** of a face, then another on the same
face, and the face splits along that line. The point under the cursor is drawn
live — a filled dot for a new corner mid-edge, a ring for an existing one — so
you aim the cut before you make it rather than discovering it afterwards.

The interesting part is what happens to the neighbours. Splitting an edge without
telling the face on its other side leaves a **T-junction**: the floor still spans
the full edge while the wall now has a corner half way along it, and the
rasteriser draws a hairline crack down the seam. So the cut carries into every
face using those edges — they gain the same corner and stay welded. Both halves
inherit the face's material slot.

After a cut the knife **keeps going** from the corner it just made, so a groove
walks across a face — or out of one half into the next — in one gesture. Esc ends
the cut, Esc again puts the knife away.

A cut that can't divide a face (two points on one edge, two corners already
joined by one) is refused with the reason and changes nothing — including the
half of it that had already been applied, which is why the whole cut is computed
on a copy and committed only if it works.

## Selecting stopped being a click-at-a-time job

**Box-select now starts wherever you press** — including on the mesh. It used to
require empty space to start from, and a blockout fills the screen, so most of
the time there was nowhere to begin: picking a row of faces meant clicking them
one by one. A press now only anchors; the release decides whether the gesture was
a click (pick what's under it) or a drag (apply the box).

**Shift adds, Ctrl removes.** Both used to mean "toggle", which is fine for one
click and useless for a box — dragging over a region you'd half-selected flipped
the overlap back off. One place reads the modifiers now, so what a click does and
what a box does can't drift apart.

**Select All / None / Invert** are on the viewport strip, one click away, and the
All button says what it will select — "All faces", "All vertices", "All edges",
following the mode you're in. Invert (`\`) is new.

## The sub-object mode is on screen

Vertex / edge / face was one cycling word in the corner of the viewport strip and
a "Tab cycles" tooltip. It is now **three chips** — ◆ vertex, ╱ edge, ◼ face —
so the mode you want is one click and the two you aren't in are visible rather
than somewhere in a rotation. Every chip names its own key (`J` / `K` / `M`); the
direct binds existed all along and nothing in the UI mentioned them.

Switching still **converts** the selection rather than dropping it: pick a face,
press `J`, and you're holding its four corners.

## Blockouts take the paint brush

Map meshes are paintable — both kinds:

- **Vertex paint** — colour per corner, the cheap retro look. Shade a corridor
  darker toward its end, warm a floor near a light, tint one wall.
- **Texture paint** — a resolution-independent overlay, so detail doesn't depend
  on how many faces the wall has. A dab paints in world space across faces, so
  one stroke down a wall-floor seam shades both surfaces at once: painted ambient
  occlusion, the baked retro look, with no bake.

The hard part isn't painting them; it's that a map mesh **re-triangulates from
scratch every time you pull a face**. Vertex paint is one colour per render
vertex and texture paint is one patch per render triangle, so after an extrude
the old block is just numbers with nothing to attach to. Applied blind it lands
on whatever now occupies those indices — paint on the wrong surfaces. Dropping it
instead is honest and infuriating: touching one wall would clear a level.

So every render vertex and triangle carries a **durable name**: its face
(identified by the set of corners it uses, which survives the face reindexing
that deleting and cutting both do) plus its corner, or its index in that face's
fan. After a rebuild the old names are matched to the new ones and the paint
follows. Move a vertex, assign a slot, cut a face somewhere else, delete one —
every surviving surface keeps its paint. A face whose corners genuinely changed
(the top of an extrusion, the two halves of a cut) comes back unpainted, because
it is a new surface.

Two things worth knowing:

- **Undo restores geometry, not paint.** Undoing an extrude brings the face back;
  the paint it lost when it became new geometry doesn't come with it.
- A blockout's paint atlas is a *dynamic* mesh slot, and its texture is rewritten
  in place when it can be. Static meshes and textures can never be freed, so the
  obvious "re-register it" would have leaked one per edit of a painted wall, for
  the whole session.

Scene load now adopts **map geometry before paint**, which it has to: paint is
keyed to a triangulation, and loading it before the geometry it belongs to found
nothing to attach to and quietly dropped it.

## Upgrading

Additive. No script, file-format or wire changes; existing `.map.ron` sidecars and
paint files load unchanged. The Map tool's two new commands (`/` knife, `\`
invert) take keys nothing else used, and every binding is still rebindable in the
Map panel with conflicts refused at bind time.

There is a new user guide: [docs/map-tools.md](../map-tools.md).

Internally, `floptle_map::SlotMesh` gained a `vert_src` field (the `(face,
vertex)` behind every emitted corner) — a struct-literal break for anything
constructing one by hand.

**1290 tests**, workspace clippy clean, seven of the eight solar smoke tests
passing (`smoke_vessel` fails on a clean tree too — game content, untouched here).
