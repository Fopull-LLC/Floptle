# Floptle v0.117.0-rc9 — "Whole Worlds" (beta)

Everything in rc8, plus big terrain edits and marks that no longer stall the
frame, planets that give their memory back, wheels that roll, and posts the
crew cannot walk through.

## Big edits stay smooth

- A large dig or flatten is made in the background and lands whole a frame or
  two later, so one big edit no longer freezes the game. Anything that reads
  the ground in between sees it as it was before the edit.
  `terrain.pending()` counts it until it lands.
- `decals.add` returns at once. A big mark is laid over the next few frames
  within a small budget each, and `decals.landed(id)` says when it is down.
  A dig re-lays only the marks it reached.
- Landing on a new world no longer hitches for seconds: far ground is put away
  a little at a time, and chunks are prepared off the frame. The time it
  takes shows as `terrainStreaming` in `perf.hitches`.
- On Linux, memory a planet no longer needs is handed back to the system, so
  a game's footprint falls when ground is put away.
- A planet generated over an existing terrain no longer shows grey squares
  or the inside of the far side of the world.

## Physics

- Free wheels roll. A plane's ground roll and a coasting vehicle no longer
  drag as if their brakes were on, and a driven wheel can no longer pull its
  craft faster than its motor allows.
- A capsule (a walking crew member) can no longer pass through a post or pole
  narrower than its own height, moving or still.
- Many moving parts touching each other cost far less: 1,000 cost about a
  hundredth of what they did.
- `spawn(prefab, pos, fn, { body = false })` spawns a prefab with no physics,
  for parts that are only seen.

## Scripting

- A script that runs past its time budget once is skipped for that frame and
  runs again the next. Only three overruns in a row stop it until it is
  edited, so a single slow frame no longer stops a script for the rest of a
  game.
- `input.simulate` numbers mouse buttons as `input.button` does (0 left, 1
  right, 2 middle), takes `mouse`, `scroll` and `text`, and a simulated click
  reaches the game's UI.
- `scene.load(name, { additive = true, parent = node })` places a scene
  under a node, where it rides along as the node moves.
- Turning a camera on from its Camera component's `active` turns the others
  off, as `setCamera` does. The component also exposes `projection`.
- `draw.setFrame(node)` draws debug lines and shapes in a node's space, so
  they stay on it however fast it moves.
- `draw.text` and `draw.rect` land in the right place in a `shot` or capture
  of a different size, `shot --after` waits for models still loading, and
  `perf.scriptMs` reads real values in `shot`.

## Plants and materials

- Scattered props give way to walkers only when their source has `bend`
  above 0 (`scatter.create{ bend = 1 }` for grass), so rocks stay put. Bent
  props now spring back upright once the walker has passed.
- Materials take a **near fade**: a surface dissolves as the camera comes
  close, between two distances (`nearFadeStart`, `nearFadeEnd`). Dithered and
  fading surfaces keep their full shadow.
- A material faded below full opacity and then brought back to 1 draws as it
  did before the fade, solid or cut out, instead of staying blended.

## Upgrading

Projects upgrade themselves when you open them; nothing to do. Upgrading no
longer rewrites scenes, prefabs or materials to record their blend mode: it
changed nothing about how they draw, and dropped the comments in them. A
project's own settings are still written to `project.ron`. What upgrading
cannot change for you:

- Scatter sources that should part around walkers need `bend = 1`; it was on
  for every source in rc7 and rc8.
- `input.simulate{ button = 1 }` now means the right button; use 0 for left.
