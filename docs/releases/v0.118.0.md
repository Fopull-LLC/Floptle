# Floptle v0.118.0 — "Full Speed"

A big step for frame rates. Forests, fog, shadows and upscaling cost a
fraction of what they did, so a dense planet at High settings fits in 60
frames a second on a mid-range card. Terrain gets real materials: every
texture slot can carry its own Material, normal, ORM and height maps, and
borders between materials that can be soft, sharp, pixel-stepped or decided
by height. Plus glass, wheels that grip, scroll views and much more.

## Faster frames

- **Leaves, grass and other cut-out cards are drawn once per pixel.** Cards
  hidden behind other cards cost nothing to shade, even on a planet. A
  forest that took 18 ms of GPU time now takes about 14.
- **Volumetric fog can be built in a grid** at a fraction of the cost of
  marching it per pixel, with the same look. Choose it under Project
  Settings ▸ Volumetric fog.
- **FSR 1 upscaling.** Render the 3D scene below full resolution and bring it
  back up sharp, in well under half a millisecond at 1440p. Choose it under
  Project Settings ▸ Upscale, with `app.setRenderScale` for the scale.
- **Shadows cost what they show.** The sun's shadow map draws only what lies
  inside it. A scattered field can cast no shadow, or none past a distance,
  with `castShadow` and `shadowDistance` on `scatter.create`.
- **Lamps out of reach cost nothing per pixel**, and ambient occlusion
  switched off costs nothing at all.
- **A single star lights the world for what the sun costs.**
- **See where the time goes.** The GPU profiler times every post effect and
  offscreen view on its own line, and `perf.counts()` reports triangles,
  shadow-map draws and terrain meshes still to come.
- **Settings a game can offer its players:** `app.setTextureQuality`,
  `app.setAntialias` (FXAA), `terrain.setDetail` for terrain view distance,
  and a camera's own `near` and `far` planes.

## Terrain materials

- **Each terrain slot can carry a Material of its own**: roughness,
  metalness, glow, tint, or unlit. Scripts read and set it with
  `terrain.slotMaterial` and `terrain.setSlotMaterial`.
- **Slots take normal, ORM and height maps**, and pixel-art slots keep them
  crisp.
- **Borders between materials have a width in metres and a style**: soft,
  sharp, wobbled, pixel-stepped, or decided by the height maps so stones
  stand out of sand. Borders run cleanly where three materials meet.
- **Textures stay steady into the distance**, can hide their repeat from far
  off, and can be larger.
- **Terrain shows its own colours from the very first frame**, instead of
  white while its pictures load.
- **Digs can be round.** Project Settings ▸ Terrain shape draws dug and
  sculpted ground smooth (Natural) or in crisp voxel facets (Faceted), and
  `terrain.setShape` changes it while a game runs.
- **Paint and digs take a brush**, soft or hard, for crisp seams and road
  edges: `terrain.paintTexture` takes a soft brush too.
- **Ravine walls are painted and solid.** No more untextured patches on
  steep walls or holes along their rims, and raycasts stop at the walls.
- **Terrain gives back the GPU memory of ground you have left behind.**

## Glass, light and effects

- **Glass reads its own shape.** A thin pane shows what is behind it
  straight through; a ball is a lens.
- **Lit particles** take a spotlight's cone and choose how strongly they
  answer lamps. Particles can draw into a render target, and
  `particles.setMax` sets one budget across every effect.
- **Effects fired from moving things** slow down and swirl the right way.
- **Reflections, motion blur and contact shadows hold steady**: reflections
  that miss turn to fine grain rather than a woven pattern, motion blur
  leaves what rides with the camera sharp, and contact shadows stay still
  while the camera barely moves.
- **Tiled materials can hide their repeat.**
- **Render-target and stacked cameras** can skip shadows, sky, terrain and
  fog, and lay themselves over after depth of field.

## Vehicles and physics

- **Wheels grip and turn the way they should**: suspension in real units
  with bump stops, the same grip forwards and backwards, held to the ground
  over crests at speed, and a project setting for wheel friction.
- **Surfaces can be slippery** with `node:setSurfaceFriction`.
- **`node.contactForce`** tells how hard the ground pushes back, and
  `assembly.impacts` says what each part of a craft hit.
- **A fast craft pays only for what it can reach**, in open air and near
  colliders alike.
- **Queries can skip a node's whole subtree** with `ignoreChildren`, and an
  ignore list skips static colliders too.

## Scripting and UI

- **Scroll views** fade the edges that hide content and can scroll to a row
  with `view:scrollTo`.
- **`ui.wantsPointer`** says when the pointer is over UI, so a click on a
  button does not also fire the game's.
- **Screen drawing stacks in the order a script makes it.**
- **What scripts hold:** `perf.scriptMemory` and `perf.resources` name the
  script behind each mesh and texture, and a script's mesh or texture can
  live as long as its node or scene.
- **Scatter** keeps the nearest props when over budget, fades props in and
  out, and answers big `scatter.near` queries flat or a page at a time.
- **Compute buffers can be atomic**, with a new tutorial: a pond the GPU
  simulates.
- **Files can be written off the frame**, so switching save slots never
  waits.
- **A game's Quit closes it**, and its resolution setting takes effect.

## Upgrading

Projects from before keep their look: they stay on marched fog, a plain
upscale and faceted digs until you choose otherwise in Project Settings. New
projects start with grid fog, FSR 1 and Natural digs. Planets in an upgraded
project are generated exactly as before; a new project generates with the
improved ravine walls.
