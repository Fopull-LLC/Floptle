# Floptle v0.117.0-rc2 — "Whole Worlds" (beta)

Planets several times the size generate in seconds, with mountain ranges,
rivers, lakes and climates. Wind and lightning, props that keep their own
look, craft that articulate, and meshes and textures a script builds itself.

## Bigger planets, made in seconds

A generated planet now costs its surface, not its volume. A 400 m world that
took nearly three minutes fills in about four seconds and keeps a third of the
memory. `deferCaves = true` leaves the buried cave zone until a dig reaches
it, so a world fills and keeps about a quarter less; with it, a 1.5 km world
fills in under a minute on a 16-core desktop. Two players' games agree on every
cave either way.

`terrain.generatePlanet` takes new layers: ridged mountain ranges, warped and
terraced ground, plateaus, overhangs and arches, steep-walled ravines, and
rivers that run downhill in valleys they carve. `biomes` paints the surface by
latitude, altitude, slope, moisture and nearness to a river.
`terrain.planetRivers(opts)` and `terrain.planetLakes(opts)` say where the
rivers run and where lakes would settle, without generating anything.
[docs/planet-generator.md](../planet-generator.md) has five example worlds to
start from.

## Ground that looks the way it collides

Terrain within 64 m of the camera is always drawn at full detail, and nothing
within 120 m is drawn coarser than half detail, so mountains no longer appear
only once you walk into them and pits are always drawn. Coarse ground far away
settles onto the real surface instead of floating metres above it. Undug ground
shows its own texture, not the subsoil under it.

A dig no longer stalls the frame: collision is rebuilt in the background and
the old ground holds until the new one lands. A ray past a planet costs about
what a ray into it does, so a script can cast several a frame.

## Wind and lightning

`weather.addWind{ velocity = vec3(4, 0, 0), gust = 0.4, turbulence = 0.2 }`
declares a wind, and `weather.windAt(point)` reads it back. A wind given a
`parent` turns with its planet. One given a `center` and `radius` is a local
cell, a storm or a valley's down-draught, that adds to the winds under it.
Gusts travel downwind, so one is seen crossing a field instead of everything
moving at once.

`weather.bolt(from, to)` gives a lightning bolt as strands to draw, a jagged
channel with forks. The same seed gives the same bolt on every machine.

## Scattered props look and behave like the model

Props now draw from their model's own colour, maps and glow, and a source's
`tint` and `emissive` shade them instead of replacing them, so one model serves
a field of any colour. A source on a planet lands on the right face of it.
Instance ids survive a round trip through Lua, so `scatter.remove` takes the
prop `scatter.near` found. Props settle a few at a time, so walking into a
field is smooth, and a script's sources are freed when it is hot-reloaded or
its node is destroyed. "Smooth + mipmaps" on a model's textures now builds the
mip chain, so far textures stop shimmering.

## Craft that articulate

Either end of a joint can be an assembly, so a vessel built from several
assemblies moves: hinges, pistons, rotors, robotic arms. A hinge or slider
motor can drive to a goal, set from a script each tick with `Joint.goal`. A
wheel hung from an assembly grips the ground and parks on a slope.
`assembly.grip` welds a craft to the ground under it, or to another body.

A part's mass is readable and writable from Lua, and the assembly re-weighs
itself on the next tick. A parked assembly falls asleep and costs nothing, and
a fast one stops at the ground instead of tunnelling in and being thrown out.

## Meshes and textures from a script

`mesh.build{ positions, indices [, normals, uvs, colors] }` builds a mesh and
answers a name that goes anywhere a model path does, `node.model` and
`scatter.create` included. `assets.textureFromPixels(w, h, pixels)` makes a
texture from raw RGBA. `anim:setBones` poses many bones in one call, and
`node.name` can be assigned.

## Materials and sky

A glTF model's metallic-roughness, normal, occlusion and emissive textures
come in with it, and draw exactly as the same maps set by hand. Materials take
an emissive map. Surface shaders get `litPbr`, the built-in Physical lighting,
and the derivatives `dpdx`, `dpdy` and `fwidth`. Shots, captures and docked
Game views draw a material's maps and shading, where before they drew it flat.

A model's textures are filtered the way its file says: a texture exported with
nearest filtering keeps crisp pixels, and one exported with linear filtering
draws smooth, with mipmaps. A model's **embedded texture filter** in the
Inspector still chooses for the whole model.

On a round world with air, flat fog fills only the view inside the air, so the
sun and other planets keep their colour. The sun low in thick air turns orange
instead of fading, and a world's cloud deck can be tinted, darkened and raised
from `setCelestial`.

## Smaller things

- `app.monitors()`, `app.displayModes()` and `app.setDisplayMode{...}` let a
  game offer resolution and monitor settings, and
  `ui.engineSettings(container, "controls")` builds the engine's rebinding page
  into a game's own panel.
- `save.set` keeps tables nested 16 deep.
- Renaming, retagging or reparenting a node no longer rebuilds the scripts'
  copy of the whole scene.
- A sound or shader under a linked project folder resolves inside the project.
- Mixer changes hold across `scene.load`; `audio.tracks()` lists the mixer.
- `perf.buckets()` names `gpuWait`, `present` and `assets`, and what is left
  is `untimed`.
- In a flat 2D view, a click selects what is drawn under the cursor, not the
  tilemap behind it.
- A package's `scene.docs` and `mesh.read` are answered the next frame, even
  when it queued nothing else, and `xpcall(f, debug.traceback)` catches an
  error in a package with the stack that raised it.

## The Hub

The Hub now counts installs with an anonymous ping once a day: a random install
id made on your machine, the Hub's version, your OS and CPU type, and its update
channel. Nothing that identifies you is sent. The Hub tells you before it sends
anything, and **Settings ▸ Install ping** turns it off and deletes the id.
`FOPULL_NO_PING=1` turns it off for every Fopull app. The editor never pings.

## Upgrading

Nothing to do. Five things look or behave differently:

- A glTF part with a metallic-roughness texture now shades as Physical. Parts
  that name only a base colour draw as before.
- A model whose file says its textures are filtered linearly now draws them
  smooth, where before every model drew crisp. Blender exports linear unless an
  image's interpolation is set to **Closest**. To keep a model crisp, set its
  embedded texture filter to crisp.
- On a round world with air, flat fog no longer covers things out in space.
- `perf.ms("other")` still answers the time no bucket measured; the bucket is
  now named `untimed`.
- Joints get twice the solver passes, so they hold stiffer.
