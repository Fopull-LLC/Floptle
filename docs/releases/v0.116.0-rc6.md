# Floptle v0.116.0-rc6 — "Lands On Its Feet" (beta)

Everything in rc5, plus physics that behaves like things, joints, isometric
and hexagonal maps, particles that land on the level, and 2D lights and
shadows that do what their controls say.

## Bodies collide with each other, and turn

Two moving rigidbodies used to pass through each other, so crates never
stacked and a character could not push one. They collide now, with their
mass, bounce and friction.

A Rigidbody with **rotates** ticked is turned by what it hits: a crate tips
off a ledge, a ball rolls down a slope, and a stack of crates stands still
instead of shaking itself apart. A sprite given a body starts with it on;
every body already in a scene stays upright. From a script, `node.spin` is
how fast a body turns, in degrees a second.

A fast box or polygon body no longer comes out the far side of a thin wall,
and a crate hitting a wall square on no longer spins.

## Joints

**Add Component ▸ Physics ▸ Joint** holds a node's body to another node, or
to where it starts:

- **hinge** turns freely or between limits, and can be driven by a motor;
- **fixed** welds two bodies so they move as one;
- **distance** holds them a set length apart, **rope** only up to it;
- **spring** pulls toward its length, as stiff and damped as you set;
- **slider** runs along the other node's axis between limits;
- **wheel** hangs on a suspension and is driven round;
- **target** pulls toward a point a script sets.

A joint can break past a force, and the two bodies it joins stop colliding
with each other unless you ask. The joint goes on the moving part, so a car
is a body with a wheel joint on each wheel. Joints are drawn in the Scene
view and saved with the scene. From a script:

```lua
lamp:setJoint{ kind = "rope", to = hook, length = 4 }
door:setJoint{ kind = "hinge", to = frame, limit = true, min = 0, max = 100 }
lamp:removeJoint()
```

## Isometric and hexagonal maps

A tileset's **grid** in the ◫ Tiles tab can be **square**, **isometric**, **hex
(pointy top)** or **hex (flat top)**. Every map painted with it lays its cells
out that way. Tiles draw in their cells, the cursor picks the cell it is over,
and solid cells collide and cast shadows with their own diamond or hexagon
outline. Art taller than its cell, like an isometric block, stands up out of
it, and the nearer block covers the one behind. **cell height** sets the
cells' proportions: 0.5 for the usual 2:1 diamond.

`tm:cellAt` and `tm:worldAt` answer in diamonds and hexagons on such a map.
Isometric and hexagonal maps from Tiled now import with their shape.
Autotiling and `tm:path` still work on square neighbours.

## Particles meet the level

Tick **collides with the level** on a track in the ✱ Particles tab and its
particles stop at walls, floors and the ground instead of passing through.
They bounce, with a **bounce** and a **friction** to tune, or **die on
contact** for rain and sparks that wink out. Only Play has a level, so the
tab's preview passes through everything.

Lit particles now take the point lights near them, with their colour and
falloff. In a 2D scene they take the 2D lights that reach their layer, so
dust glows beside a torch and stays dark in a corner.

## 2D lights and shadows

- **Only solid tiles cast.** A tile layer used to throw shadows from every
  opaque pixel, decor and background included. It now casts from the tiles
  its tileset marks solid, as one block per wall, so a light finds no seams.
- **No more streaks.** Shadows from a light some way off broke into thin
  lines of light fanning out across the floor. They are clean edges now.
- **shadow strength** under 2D light is how much of a light a caster stops.
  Lower it for a shadow that lets some light through.
- **height** under 2D light sets how high a lamp hangs over normal-mapped art.
  Low lamps throw long relief and high lamps light from above. It was fixed
  at a third of the range.
- A 2D light no longer shows the 3D **casts shadows** box, which did nothing
  there. **casts stop this light** is its switch.

From a script: `node:setLighting2D{ shadowStrength = 0.5, height = 2 }`.

## Pixel-perfect 2D

Set **Camera 2D ▸ art pixels** to your art's pixels per world unit, and the
camera keeps its zoom where every art pixel covers a whole number of screen
pixels, including while a script zooms it. Play and `floptle check` name any
sprite or tile layer drawn at a different density, which is usually a node
scaled up to make a small sprite bigger. From a script:
`node:setCamera2D{ artPixels = 8 }`.

A followed player no longer wobbles a pixel as the camera tracks them, and a
camera a script moves is no longer nudged off the position it was given.

## Editing

- **The Scene view.** Press on a node that is already selected and drag, and
  it moves. A press that does not travel is still a click. A sprite, tile
  layer or 2D body only turns in its plane, and anywhere inside its ring
  turns it.
- **Tiles.** With the tile tool out, `,` and `.` turn the brush a quarter
  turn either way, and `[` and `]` mirror it.
- **The Image tab.** A marquee dragged up or to the left now selects the box
  you drew, not a strip. **Ctrl+wheel** changes the brush size one step per
  notch, and a plain scroll never does.
- **Removing things.** Any type added to a node, such as a Sprite, can be
  taken off from its header's menu with **Remove component**, leaving an
  Empty. Curve keys and gradient stops can be removed.
- **Undo.** Tileset edits in the Tiles tab and edits in the Particles tab
  undo with Ctrl+Z. Image palette changes undo too. Deleting an animation
  state asks first.

## Scripts and tools

- A tile a script sets or clears with `tm:set`, `tm:fill` or `tm:fillRect`
  collides from the next frame, and a static block a script moves takes its
  collider with it. `tm:oneWay` answers whether a square is a one-way
  platform. A body standing on a one-way ledge reads as grounded.
- A param pinned in a scene that a script has since changed to an on/off
  switch is named in the Console and by `floptle check`, where before it
  quietly arrived as off.
- `floptle run` and `floptle shot` take `--fps` to step frames at a display
  rate and `--frame-jitter` to vary each frame's time, for a bug that only
  shows on a fast monitor.

## Upgrading

Nothing to do. Bodies in your scenes stay upright unless you tick
**rotates**. A tile layer that cast shadows now casts only from its solid
tiles. If you relied on decor tiles casting, mark those tiles solid in the
Tiles tab.
