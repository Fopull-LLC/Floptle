# Floptle v0.45.0 — "Light Gets Around"

Light that bounces. Put a **Light Probes** box around a room, press Bake, and the
red wall makes the floor red, the bright window fills the corner opposite it, and
the ceiling picks up what the carpet is doing.

Until now a scene had exactly one answer for "what light reaches a surface that
the sun can't see": a single flat ambient colour, the same inside a sealed box as
in an open field. That is the thing that makes a room read as a diorama no matter
how good the materials are.

Also in this release: the default normal map was not flat. Every unmapped
material in the engine has been shading as though its surface were bent 39°.

---

## Light Probes

Add a **Light Probes** node, size the box to a room, press **☀ Bake**.

Baking renders the scene from a lattice of points inside the box and keeps, at
each one, the light arriving from every direction. Inside the box that replaces
the flat ambient. Outside it, nothing changes. A scene with no volume renders
exactly the frames it rendered before.

**Everything that can be seen contributes.** Meshes with their textures, terrain
with its splats, tilemaps, sculpted matter, characters, custom shaders, emissive
surfaces, and the sky — because a probe is a camera, and what it gathers is the
scene as it actually renders. There is nothing to mark up, nothing to tag as
"static", nothing to export.

**Bounces.** 1 is light coming off surfaces once — the difference between a black
corner and a lit one. 2 and 3 fill in soft interiors, and cost the same again
each.

**Intensity** is the artistic one, and it does not need a re-bake. Physically
correct bounce is often a little too polite for a game; past 1 is a look, not a
mistake. Same for **leak rejection** and the **surface offset** — drag them and
the scene re-lights while you watch.

**Skip layers** keeps things that move out of the bake. A character should not be
baked into the light it happens to be standing in, because it will still be lit
by that light after it walks away.

The bake is saved next to the scene as a `.fgi` and loads with it. It ships with
your game.

### Not glowing through the wall

The failure everyone recognises from probe-based lighting is the lit room next
door leaking faintly through the wall between them. Two things stop it here.

Every probe records the closest surface it can see, so a probe buried inside a
wall is *recognisable as buried* and is thrown away rather than averaged in.
That's the **leak rejection** knob — turn it up in tight interiors, down if you
want the contact bounce in a cupboard back.

And a probe on the wrong side of the surface being shaded is weighted away, so
light arrives from the directions it could actually have come from.

### Seeing what you baked

- **Show only the bounce** — every direct light off, so what is left on screen is
  exactly what was baked. The fastest way to tell a dark bake from a dark scene.
- **Show the probes** — each one drawn in the colour it baked, and the ones the
  leak test rejected drawn hollow. A grid too coarse for the room, a volume
  nudged half out of the level, or a row of probes sunk into the floor are all
  invisible in the finished picture and obvious here.

The Inspector says how many probes your settings will bake **before** you press
the button, because that number is the difference between four seconds and four
minutes.

### From a script

```lua
local gi = find("GI"):getcomponent("LightProbes")

-- the flashback: real light, no bounce
gi.intensity = 0

-- and back, over a second
gi.intensity = lerp(gi.intensity, 1, dt * 1)
```

## The default normal map was not flat

A normal map, a roughness map, a metallic map and an occlusion map are not
pictures. They are numbers that happen to be stored in an image, and Floptle was
putting all four through the sRGB curve on the way in — the transform that exists
so *photographs* look right.

For the flat default that meant (128, 128, 255) — the "no bump here" value — was
arriving at the shader as a surface tilted **39 degrees**. Every material without
a normal map in every scene has been lit slightly wrong since surface maps
shipped, and it was invisible, because a uniform tilt still looks like a lit
object: the highlight is just in the wrong place, on everything, consistently.

Fixed. Surface maps are read as the numbers they are; base colour is still read
as the picture it is. Your existing normal maps will read stronger and correct,
roughness and metallic will match the values you set, and unmapped surfaces are
now lit by their own geometry.

It was found by the new GI probe, which is the first thing in the engine that
measures a shading normal directly instead of looking at a highlight and judging
it plausible. `pbr_probe` now asserts it against arithmetic — a face lit head-on
must be lit as `N·L = 1` — so it cannot come back.

## Nothing to do on upgrade

A scene with no Light Probes node renders exactly what it rendered before, and
saves exactly the file it saved before.

Surfaces **will** look different where a normal, roughness, metallic or occlusion
map is in play, because they were being read wrong. If a material was tuned
around the old behaviour, its roughness is the knob to revisit.

## Not in this one

- **Per-surface lightmaps.** A probe grid cannot represent a shadow sharper than
  its own spacing; the fine contact detail is still SSAO's job. A real lightmap
  needs every surface unwrapped into a shared atlas, which is a pipeline of its
  own.
- **More than one volume per scene.** A second one is ignored rather than
  fighting the first.
- **Real-time GI.** Baked is what this release is. The renderer's seam is a
  single function, so a live source can take its place later without anything
  above it changing.
