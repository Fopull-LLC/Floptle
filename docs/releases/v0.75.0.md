# Floptle v0.75.0 — "Own Look"

A part of a model can finally wear its own shader, two Inspector panels stay
fully on screen instead of running off the edge, and the shader language
picked up the math vocabulary other engines already have.

## A part's own shader actually draws now

Giving one part of a model its own material — the **override** button next
to a part in ◑ Model materials, or **◑ Override material for this object**
in the Inspector — let you pick a custom shader for it, but the shader never
took effect: the part kept its plain material look no matter what was set,
and the shader panel got stuck reading "compiling…" forever. A part's own
shader now compiles and draws correctly, independently of whatever shader
(if any) the rest of the model is wearing — so one arm can be glass while
the rest of the character stays ordinary.

## Sliders stay inside the panel

A slider with a caption — bounce, friction, radius, and dozens more across
the Inspector — could draw its label past the edge of a narrow panel instead
of shrinking to fit, most visible in the Rigidbody component's stack of
sliders. Captions now shrink to an ellipsis before they'd run off the side,
the same way every other label in the Inspector already does.

## New shader math: lerp, remap, rotate, refract

The shader graph and the `.flsl` language gained five functions: `lerp`
(the same blend `mix` already did, under the name most people reach for
first), `inverseLerp` and `remap` for turning one range of values into
another — health into a fade, distance into brightness — `rotate2D` for
spinning a UV or a point without hand-building the matrix, and `refract`
alongside the existing `reflect` for bending a ray through a surface.
