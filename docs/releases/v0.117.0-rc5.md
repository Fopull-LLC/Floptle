# Floptle v0.117.0-rc5 — "Whole Worlds" (beta)

Everything in rc3, plus craft that fly low without dragging the frame down,
grips that hold on a moving planet, and big worlds that load and land without
stalling.

## Flying low stays fast

A craft flying near a planet could cost 40 to 60 ms of physics a frame, enough
to drop a game from 60 frames a second to under 10 on a climb. Near ground the
engine looked for the closest surface even in open air, where there was none.
Now it doesn't, and an assembly only checks the colliders near it. The same
climb costs a few milliseconds.

## Grips and gravity hold on a moving planet

- `assembly.grip` holds. In rc3 a craft moved by `assembly.teleport` still read
  its old place until the end of the frame, so a grip taken right after could
  weld it to ground far away, and it slid or tore loose. A teleported craft
  reads where it was sent at once, and a grip out of the craft's reach is
  refused with a warning.
- When the world recentres around a far-travelling player, a planet's gravity
  and its water move with it, so "down" no longer swings for a tick.
- Water declared with `water.addVolume` follows its planet every physics step,
  so things float on a lake on a moving world.
- `raycast`, `spherecast` and `overlapSphere` see each part of an assembly, and
  `ignore` passes through the whole craft.
- A joint keeps its rest pose when the craft around it is rebuilt.

## Big worlds load and land smoothly

- A planet saved by an older version now saves small after its next save, as
  rc3 promised. A planet with edits loads in the background, and a large one
  resumes in about half the time.
- A generated world no longer freezes the frame for seconds as it finishes.
- Scattered props settle with the ground's height read straight from the
  terrain, so walking or flying low through a field stays smooth.
- `perf.hitches()` rows carry `parts`, which name the work inside a slow frame:
  `collisionRebuild`, `terrainEdits` and `planetLanded`.
- Undug ground shows only its own surface material. The patches of subsoil that
  showed through are gone.

## Plants, beams and decals

- Scattered props wear their prefab's material, so **sway**, **backlight** and
  colour reach every plant, and a tree crossing between detail levels no longer
  looks see-through.
- Jagged beams join cleanly at every kink, so a lightning bolt's glow is one
  ribbon.
- A decal on terrain stays on the ground when the ground under it is dug.
- Surface shaders can read `vertexColor` and `skyRadiance(dir, roughness)`, and
  a lake's `water.surface` stays inside its basin.

## Smaller things

- Upgrading a project leaves effect files alone unless they really need
  migrating, and says which files it changed.
- A number in a script's scene parameters, such as a seed, reads back exactly.
- `scatter.near` returns where a prop landed, with `settled = true`.

## Upgrading

Nothing to do. Two things look or behave differently:

- A world generated with `caveDepth = 0` now shows its subsoil and rock layers
  under the surface. Before, everything under the surface was deep rock.
- `assembly.grip` refuses a point more than one unit beyond the craft's parts.
