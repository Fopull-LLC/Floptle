# Floptle v0.46.0 — "Something In The Air"

Fog you can light. Turn one slider up and the sun standing behind a fog bank
lights it, a torch carried into it glows around itself, and a shadow crossing it
stays a shadow — which is where beams through a window come from.

Until now fog was a colour the picture faded toward. It was the same grey whether
the sun was ahead of you or behind you, and a lamp inside it did nothing at all.

---

## Lit fog

On the Lighting node, with fog set to **volumetric**:

- **lit by the scene** — 0 is the flat colour it always was, 1 is fog lit by the
  sun, by every point light, and by the baked bounce. Past 1 exaggerates, which
  is a look and not a mistake.
- **forward scatter** — which way the air throws light. Turn it up and looking
  toward the sun through the layer blooms; looking away stays dim. That asymmetry
  is most of why lit fog reads as weather rather than as a brighter wash.
- **shafts** — shadows *inside* the fog. This is the beam through the window, the
  light between the trees, the god ray off the edge of a building.
- **quality** — how many samples each pixel takes. It changes how smoothly the
  fog resolves and not how bright it is, so it is safe to drop on a weak machine.
- **max distance** — how far a ray that hits nothing keeps marching.

Once light is injected, the **fog colour becomes what the media is made of**
rather than what it looks like. Warm fog under a blue sun is both. Fog in a room
with no light in it is dark, because there is nothing for it to scatter.

### Fog now reaches the sky

Volumetric fog composites over the sky. It always should have — a fog layer is
something a ray really passes through on its way out of the world, and leaving it
out is what put a hard line at the horizon and hid every beam that had sky behind
it rather than a wall.

The depth-ramp mode still leaves the sky crisp. That one is a distance ramp, not
weather, and fogging a skybox with it just washes it out.

### From a script

```lua
local env = find("Lighting"):getcomponent("Light")

-- the storm arrives: the air starts carrying the light
env.fogLight = lerp(env.fogLight, 2.2, dt * 0.5)
env.fogDensity = lerp(env.fogDensity, 0.08, dt * 0.5)

-- and the cheap seats: lit, but nothing carves it
env.fogShafts = 0
```

`fogLight`, `fogAnisotropy`, `fogSteps` and `fogShafts` join the fog set already
there.

## On upgrade

A scene with fog off, or fog set to **depth**, renders exactly what it rendered
before.

A scene already using **volumetric** fog will look different, and better: it
arrives lit, with shafts on and scattering forward, because fog that ignores the
sun standing behind it is the thing that made volumetric mode read as a grey
wash. If you want the old appearance back exactly, set **lit by the scene** to 0
— that is not an approximation of the previous look, it is the same arithmetic.

Watch the frame rate the first time. Shafts cost a shadow trace at every sample
along every pixel, which is the whole price of the effect and the whole reason
there is a beam. Turning shafts off, or the quality down, are both real dials.

## Not in this one

- **Fog you can place.** One layer, scene-wide, bounded by height. A box of fog
  you can drop in one room is the obvious next shape.
- **Thick-cloud glow.** Light spreads *through* real fog, not just along the ray
  you are looking down. Forward scatter gets most of that look; the rest is a
  much heavier pass.
- **Particles in the lit media.** Smoke and dust still fade with distance rather
  than picking up the light in the air around them.
