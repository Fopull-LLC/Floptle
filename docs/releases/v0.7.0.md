# Floptle v0.7.0 — "First Draft"

Build the level in the engine. This release adds a full map-building suite:
draw a shape by dragging out its footprint and then its height, push its faces
around with normal-aligned gizmos, extrude and inset it into a room, and give
individual faces their own materials — without leaving the editor and without a
Blender round trip. A blockout is the first draft of a level; now you can write
it where you'll play it.

Everything here is a generic engine feature. The kernel is a standalone crate
(`floptle-map`, 49 unit tests) with no engine dependencies beyond glam.

## Draw shapes, don't spawn them

- **Drag out the base, then the height.** Press **8** for the ⬢ Map tool, pick
  a shape, drag its footprint on the ground — or on any map surface you aim at,
  so you can build straight onto the wall you just made — release, move to set
  the height, click. A live wireframe of the real shape and a
  `4.00 x 3.00 x 6.00 · 8 steps` readout follow the cursor the whole way, and
  the shape stays armed so the next drag draws another one.
- **Seven shapes**: box, plane, wedge (ramp), cylinder, sphere, stairs, arch.
- **Drawn at any proportion.** A cylinder or sphere on a non-square footprint
  comes out as an honest ellipse or ellipsoid — real geometry, not a scaled
  node, so its textures keep their real-world scale.
- **Stairs and ramps climb the way you dragged them**, with a green arrow
  labelled "up" on the footprint while you draw *and* whenever such a node is
  selected — so which way it goes is never a guess.
- **Turn it as you go.** `,` and `.` turn the shape 90° left/right and `Z` turns
  it around, all inside the footprint you dragged (a quarter turn swaps the
  extents so the shape still fills the rectangle). `[` and `]` retune the
  resolution — stair steps, cylinder sides, arch segments — with the preview
  updating live. `Esc` cancels.
- Everything grid-snaps when snap is on: the footprint, the height, and
  sub-object drags.

## Edit the geometry

- **Vertex / edge / face modes.** Click to select, Shift or Ctrl to add, drag
  empty space to box-select, and a plain click on another node switches to it.
  **Tab** cycles the mode and *converts* your selection rather than dropping it
  — pick a face, press Tab, and you're holding its four verts.
- **Selection reaches only what you can see.** No more grabbing the vertex on
  the far side of a wall; a toggle turns it off when you want to.
- **Move, rotate and scale the selection**, with **normal / local / global**
  handle orientation. `normal` is the default and it's the difference between a
  modeling tool and a toy: a diagonal face's handles point straight out of that
  face, so it extrudes in one drag instead of two axis drags that only
  approximate it. Grid snap moves it in clean steps *along that axis*.
- **Modeling ops**: extrude, inset (inset then extrude carves a recess),
  subdivide, bridge two faces with a tube of walls, weld, delete faces, split
  the selection off into its own node, flip faces, flip the whole mesh, snap to
  grid.
- **Selection ops**: all, none, grow, connected, coplanar, by material slot, and
  quad edge loops.
- **Undo is one step per gesture**, and your sub-object selection survives it —
  extrude, undo, adjust, extrude again without reselecting.

## Shapes stay parametric

A drawn shape remembers what it is, so you can change its **step count, sides,
rings or arch opening after the fact** — from the Map tab's SHAPE panel, or with
`[` / `]` straight on the selected node. Re-generating keeps your material slot
names, and keeps the per-face assignments too when the face count still lines
up. Resizing keeps it parametric; the first op that *moves geometry* retires the
parameters, because silently re-generating would throw that edit away.

## Size, pivot and materials

- **Type exact extents.** The SIZE panel resizes the *geometry*, not the node
  scale — so textures keep their real-world scale instead of stretching.
- **Pivot controls**: centre the pivot in the mesh, or drop it onto the current
  selection (the point rotation and scale then work around).
- **Per-face materials in one click.** *◑ New material for selected faces* makes
  a slot from your selection and gives it its own material. Slots resolve
  through the existing per-object material system, so the full material editor —
  colour, texture, tiling, shader — works on them, per node instance.
- **Solid and textured by default.** Every shape spawns `Collidable` (its exact
  triangulation bakes into the static trimesh collider on Play) and carries a
  dev-grid texture whose UVs are 1 unit = 1 tile, so the grid measures the room
  for you while you block it out. Untick either in the Inspector.

## Every control has a hotkey, and every hotkey is yours

**41 commands** — draw each shape, resolution ±, the turns, the sub-object
modes, every selection op, the gizmo modes and handle orientation, every modify
op, the pivots, new-material-from-selection — all bound, all rebindable from the
Map tab's KEYS section, all persisted per user.

Conflicts are prevented rather than discouraged. Map chords are only consulted
while the Map tool is active, you're not typing, Ctrl is up, Play is stopped and
no timeline has focus — so they can't reach an application shortcut. Inside that
context the editor still owns the fly camera, the tool digits, F/Q/G/H, Escape,
F1/F2, the arrows and Enter; those handlers ignore modifiers, so those keys are
reserved with *every* modifier and rebinding onto one is refused with the reason
("W is the fly camera"). So is a chord another map command already holds. Even a
hand-edited preferences file can't create a conflict.

## It survives everything you'll throw at it

- **Prefabs and the clipboard carry the geometry.** Copy a wall into another
  scene, another project, or save it as a prefab — it arrives as the real shape
  with its own identity, not a placeholder.
- **Play is safe.** Map geometry is snapshotted on Play and restored on Stop,
  and editing is refused while playing (it wouldn't be undoable and the collider
  wouldn't rebuild) with a message saying so.
- **A sidecar it can't read is never overwritten.** If `maps/<scene>.map.ron`
  fails to load, map nodes stay empty and saving map geometry is disabled until
  it's fixed — rather than filling the level with placeholder cubes and making
  that permanent on the next Ctrl+S.
- **No T-junctions**, enforced by a kernel test over every primitive at a spread
  of parameters. A vertex sitting in the middle of another face's edge looks
  watertight in a render and tears the mesh apart the moment you drag that face.
  Subdividing part of a mesh stitches the same corner into the neighbours it
  borders, for the same reason.
- **Absurd proportions are just proportions.** A sweep builds every shape across
  extents from 0 to 5000 in every axis and asserts none panic, none produce a
  non-finite vertex, and none come out invalid.

## The editor around it

- **A new default layout**, grouped by what each dock is for: Hierarchy + ⬢ Map
  on the left (what the scene contains and what you build it from), Scene / Game
  / Scripting / ◈ Shaders / ◎ Controller in the centre (viewports and the
  full-canvas editors that replace them), Inspector / Δ Terrain / 🖌 Paint on the
  right (properties of the selection), Assets / Console / ✏ Animating /
  ✨ Particles / 🎧 Mixer below (the project and the timelines that scrub it).
  **Window ▸ ⟲ Reset layout** returns to it at any time.
- **The Map tab** is laid out as titled sections in the order you work —
  DRAW, SELECT, TRANSFORM, MODIFY, SHAPE, SIZE, FACE MATERIALS, KEYS — with a
  left label column so controls line up, equal-width chips for anything that
  picks a mode, equal-width buttons for anything that acts, and the rarely-touched
  knobs behind disclosures.
- **A one-line status strip in the viewport** states the current mode, gizmo and
  handle orientation plus what the next click and drag will do. Each word is
  clickable to cycle; `⌄` opens a shape picker. It states the tool's mode
  without duplicating the panel or covering the scene.
- **Ops explain themselves.** Anything that declines says why in the Console —
  "select one or more FACES first", "those two faces have different corner
  counts", "nothing merged — no two selected verts are within 0.050 of each
  other".

## Fixes

- **The Scripting IDE no longer crashes on Ctrl+X.** Cutting a line re-entered
  the egui context from inside an input-state lock and deadlocked the editor.
- **Copy and cut reach the text field again.** The IDE was swallowing both
  before the editor widget saw them; it now only intercepts them for the
  whole-line copy/cut, when nothing is selected.
- **The play-mode cursor stays where you can use it.** A mouse-locking script
  hid the cursor everywhere, including over the Inspector; it now hides only
  over the game view.
- **The animator says what you meant.** A mistyped method (`anim:IsPlaying`)
  now raises a case-insensitive did-you-mean instead of a bare nil-call error,
  and `docs/scripting.md` documents Lua's `and`/`or` conditional idiom — with
  the one gotcha that trips people, plus `anim:current` as an alias of
  `anim:state`.
