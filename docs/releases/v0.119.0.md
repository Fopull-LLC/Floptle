# Floptle v0.119.0 — "Open Water"

Water you can build a game around. Seas carry real swells that float boats
and draw the same waves you see. Lakes fill, spill and settle, and rivers can
be laid down as a line of points. Water on a round world stays where it lies,
and everything it touches can hear the splash. Rigged models get spring
bones, so hair, cables and tails swing on their own. Models with many
pictures draw as one, and a busy scene costs far less CPU per frame.

## Water

- **Swells.** `water.setSwell` gives a body of water Gerstner waves. The
  physics floats things on the same waves the shader draws, and
  `water.swellAt` tells a script the height and slope at any point.
- **Simulated water fills, flows and dries out.** Lakes fill and spill over
  their edges. Water can be added at runtime with `water.addWater`, saved
  with the game, and lie on a round world, stored only where it is.
- **A lake held at its level sleeps** with `water.hold`. One that leaks at an
  edge hands its fill over and goes quiet, so still water costs next to
  nothing. `water.stats` reports what simulated water costs a frame.
- **Rivers as ribbons**: a line of points with a width, a depth and a current.
  A ribbon lies flat unless it belongs to a world.
- **`water.setLevel`** raises or lowers a body of water in place.
- **Scripts hear splashes.** `water.splashes` reports what entered the water
  and how hard, and `water.setSlam` lets a fast entry stop hard.
- **Water's surface comes with its depth, flow and foam**, for shaders and
  scripts that want them.
- **Underwater sounds right.** `audio.setWorldLowpass` muffles the world in
  one call and leaves the helmet's own sounds clear. Water's built-in
  underwater look can be switched off for a game that draws its own.
- **Ground seen through water** is told apart from bare ground in screen
  shaders, and far ground under a sea stays under it.

## Spring bones

- **Hair, cables and tails swing on their own.** Give a rigged model spring
  chains in the Inspector or with `anim:setSpringBones`, and they follow the
  model's motion with stiffness, gravity and collision.
- Chains can stiffen at the root, slide along colliders, be set up from
  patterns, and come with defaults stored in the model.
- `anim:setSpringEnabled`, `anim:setSpringGravity` and `anim:resetSprings`
  change them while a game runs.

## Faster frames

- **Models whose parts wear different pictures draw as one instance**,
  through texture arrays built off the frame, instead of one draw per
  material.
- **Much less CPU per frame.** Reading a component costs a fraction of what
  it did, a deep node draws for what a loose one does, `find()` costs the
  same in any size of scene, and scripts with nothing to do cost next to
  nothing.
- **Terrain draws share a few large buffers** and skip repeated work, and a
  card that runs out of video memory sheds terrain detail instead of
  raising errors.
- **Terrain memory comes back** after a walk round a small world.
- **A game run with `floptle play` stops paying for editor panels** it never
  shows.
- **See where the time goes.** A frame reports how much CPU its main thread
  used and how long the script collector ran, slow frames name their phase,
  and `perf.counts()` names what scripts passed by.

## Light, fog and shadows

- **Sun shadows hold still** while the camera moves, and a low sun no longer
  stripes the floors it lights.
- **Lit fog** takes separate sun, lamp and ambient strengths, a ceiling, and
  a share per lamp, and can take its colour as the look rather than as an
  albedo.
- **Lamps fade at the edge of the sixteen nearest** instead of blinking.
- **Ground and water seen low across keep their detail** with anisotropic
  filtering, 8x by default. Choose it under Project Settings or with
  `app.setAnisotropy` from a settings screen.
- **Cut-out pictures keep their coverage** into the distance.
- A shader that lights itself can darken under cover and take a lamp's light.

## Scripting

- **Checkpoints.** A game can checkpoint its save, ground included, and go
  back to it. `save.peek` reads a saved table without copying it,
  `save.version` says when a key changed, and `save.copySlot` duplicates a
  slot.
- **Ground queries in bulk.** `terrain.surfaceBelow` finds the ground under
  thousands of points in one call. `terrain.editsSince` says where the
  ground changed, and `terrain.layersAt` says which relief layer shaped a
  place on a generated planet.
- **A smooth simulation clock.** `simTime` and `simDt` read the physics
  clock, which runs smooth above the tick rate, and Project Settings ▸ Game ▸
  Script clock can make it what `time` and `dt` follow.
- **Sound.** `audio.duration` says how long a clip is, `sound:audible` how
  loud it reaches the listener. Sounds can share a voice budget per group
  and grow duller with distance.
- **Meshes and shaders.** `mesh.update` rewrites a built mesh in place, a
  script can put a `.flsl` shader on a model it built, and screen shaders
  read textures, lists, the sky and a pixel's world point.
- **A script that runs past its budget is the only one stopped**, and a
  stalled machine no longer stops a script for running out of time.
- **Time warp** drops before a coasting craft meets the ground ahead.
- **A bad number from a script can no longer hang physics**, and a terrain
  query near a chunk edge no longer crashes the game.

## Upgrading

Projects from before play as they did. Water stays still until you give it
a swell or a simulation, spring bones are off until a model has chains, and
`time` and `dt` keep following the frame (Project Settings ▸ Game ▸ Script
clock; new projects start on the simulation clock). The one
visible change is sharper distant ground from 8x anisotropic filtering; set
Texture filtering to 1x in Project Settings for the old look. An upgraded
project with a long shadow reach keeps its single shadow map.
