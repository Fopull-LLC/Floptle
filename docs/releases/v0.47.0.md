# Floptle v0.47.0 — "Lights With A Size"

A light can now be a shape. A window, a strip light, a bulb with an actual bulb —
and the room knows the difference, because a four-metre window lights a wall
evenly where a point at its centre leaves a hot spot in the middle and darkness
at both ends.

Nothing in the world is a mathematical point. That was the one approximation
underneath every highlight in the engine, and it is the reason a shiny surface
always caught a pinprick of light instead of the shape of the thing lighting it.

---

## Emitters

Every light node gets an **emits from** setting:

- **point** — a dimensionless source. The default, and exactly the light every
  existing scene already has.
- **sphere** — a bulb with size. The highlight becomes a disc, the terminator
  softens.
- **rect** — a window, a softbox, a monitor. Faces the node's forward.
- **disk** — a downlight, a porthole. Faces the node's forward.
- **tube** — a strip light, a neon bar, a blade. Lies along the node's local X
  and streaks its highlight along itself.

A **rect** and a **disk** are one-sided: a window lights the room, not the wall
it is set into. Turn on *lights both ways* for a panel that glows from both
faces. Scaling the node scales the emitter.

The Scene view draws the emitter at its real size and facing, with an arrow out
of the emitting face. Worth looking at once: **a rect light aimed at the wall
behind it lights nothing**, and there is no way to see that in the finished
picture — the room is dark and the light looks like it is on.

### What actually changes

**The direction light comes from.** Stand beside a tall strip light and the light
arrives from the whole strip, not from a point in its middle. That is the
difference between a wall that reads as lit by a window and one that reads as lit
by a bulb pretending to be a window.

**The shape of a highlight.** A bar smears a long reflection along its own
length. A window leaves a broad soft rectangle. This is where the shape is most
obvious and it costs nothing extra to see it — put a glossy floor under a strip
light.

**How hard an edge is.** A big light wraps around a curve; a point light stops
dead. The terminator softens in proportion to how large the emitter looks from
where you are standing.

**Range measures to the nearest part of the light.** A three-metre bar whose
centre is out of reach still has an end beside you.

### From a script

```lua
local l = find("Window"):getcomponent("PointLight")

-- the blinds open
l.width = lerp(l.width, 3.0, dt)

-- and at night the same fitting becomes a bulb, keeping its size
l.shape = 1
```

`shape` is 0 point, 1 sphere, 2 rect, 3 disk, 4 tube, alongside `width`,
`height`, `radius`, `length`, `thickness` and `twoSided`. A dimension reads 0 on
a shape that has no such dimension.

## On upgrade

Nothing to do, and nothing looks different. Every light in every existing scene
is a point emitter, which takes the same path it always did and produces the same
number it always produced. A scene saves the same file it saved before — the
emitter is only written when it is not a point.

## Not in this one

- **Shadows shaped by the light.** A wide window still casts a shadow whose
  softness comes from the shadow settings, not from the window's own width.
- **Textured emitters.** A rect light emits one colour, not a picture.
- **The light drawing itself.** Put a mesh with an emissive material where the
  emitter is if you want to see it in frame.
