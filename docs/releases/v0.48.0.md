# Floptle v0.48.0 — "Where Things Touch"

The small dark line under a foot, in a seam, behind a bolt. Turn on **contact
shadows** and objects stop floating.

Until now a moving mesh cast its shadow through its *collider* — a box or a
capsule — so a character's shadow was a capsule's. That reads fine from across a
room and wrong from three feet away, because the place a capsule is least like a
character is exactly the place you look: where they meet the floor.

---

## Contact shadows

On the Lighting node, under shadows. Off by default; turning it on costs a short
trace per lit surface.

- **reach** — how far it looks. Short is the point. A third of a metre is a foot
  on a floor; turn it up and you start getting the whole shadow of a small
  object, at the cost of distant geometry occasionally smearing over things in
  front of it.
- **strength** — how dark it goes, before the shadow tint and strength you
  already set.
- **steps** — samples along the trace. Raise it if the shadow looks striped.

It shadows from the **real silhouette of whatever is on screen** — skinned
characters, morphing matter, tilemaps, custom shaders, anything — with nothing to
tag and nothing to bake. It also inherits your shadow tint, strength and
posterize settings, because it is the same shadow seen closer up and two shadows
with two different looks would read as two shadows.

**What it can't do**, and this is the shape of the technique rather than a bug:
it can only shadow from things that are actually on screen. Something off the
edge of the frame, or hidden behind something else, casts nothing — which is why
this is a short-range companion to the shadows you already have and not a
replacement for them.

```lua
local env = find("Lighting"):getcomponent("Light")

-- indoors, where everything is close: turn it on and reach short
env.contactShadows = 1
env.contactLength = 0.25
```

## About cascaded shadow maps

They were on the plan and they are not in this release, on purpose.

Cascades buy one thing Floptle lacks: a moving mesh's exact silhouette at *long*
range. They charge two to four extra renders of the whole scene every frame, a
cascade seam to maintain forever, and a second walk of the scene that has to stay
in step with the first — which is a mistake this engine has made four separate
times and paid for each one.

Contact shadows cover the near half of that gap for a fraction of the cost, and
that is the half you actually look at. If the far half ever matters for a
specific character, one small shadow map for that one character buys most of it
without any of the cascade machinery — and that is the recommended route, not
cascades.

## On upgrade

Nothing to do. Contact shadows are off unless you turn them on, and a scene that
does not renders exactly what it rendered before.
